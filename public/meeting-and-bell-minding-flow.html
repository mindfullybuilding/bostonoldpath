Flow of the Meeting and Bell Minding Cadences

This guidance is to support the facilitator with an example in managing the flow and cadence of a Sangha meeting. We should feel free to adjust the times as appropriate because no meeting’s the same. When the circle is small, we need less time for introductions and where there are more people, we’ll need more time for dharma sharing. You may wish to introduce mindful movement before we sit, or walking. It takes a while for us to settle into our breath after a busy day and concerns about getting to sangha. Singing is always welcome. It’s important to begin and end on time. It’s a good idea to write out your schedule and be very familiar with it.

10 minutes before the meeting starts

Be settled beforehand, seated and let us benefit from your mindfulness.

The meeting starts

Begin with a greeting and introduce yourself. If there’s a new friend, ask if they’re familiar with our tradition. If not, explain the evening, generally. Then, at each transition, offer guidance. Our goal is to make sure they feel welcome and not an “outsider.”

~3 minutes after the meeting starts

Prepare for Walking Meditation (10 minutes.) If you offer an explanation, (for instance about bowing or moving as a sangha) guidance or a gatha, it’s done before we stand. It’s helpful to offer clear instruction. Mention we stand behind our chairs and that you will invite four bells: 1) to prepare to stand; 2) to stand; 3) to bow to the sangha and 4) to turn to the left & begin walking. You can mention we walk about 10 minutes. Invite the walking bell about 15 steps before we reach our chairs & give us the time to return. When we are all back, invite the bell & bow to the sangha before we sit together.

If friends have arrived during walking wait for everyone to find a chair/cushion before inviting the bell for us to sit at the sound of the bell.

~13 minutes after the meeting starts

Sitting Meditation.

You may want to offer guidance on posture or on our breath practice as ways to stay present. Let us know if you intend to offer a guided meditation and do it at the beginning of the sit. (It’s better not to offer this practice until you’ve practiced a long while and have deep experience in the practice. It requires skill and practice beforehand.)

Mention you’ll invite three bells to begin and two at the end. When you are settled and ready, wake up the bell and then invite three full bells, with three breaths in between each. We are not in a hurry and your pace will provide ease to the sangha.

Gently wake up the bell and then invite three full bells, with 3 breaths between each. We sit in meditation about 20 minutes. End the sit by waking up the bell and then offer 2 bells, close together.

~30 minutes after the meeting starts

Introductions

Give clear guidance about the length—7 words or fewer—to allow for longer time for Dharma Sharing. Remind us to bow in with our name and it’s helpful to offer a prompt such as

Internal weather report or Something that brought us joy today or Something that is on our mind or Our intention for the evening A condition of happiness A challenge....

~35 minutes after the meeting starts

Offer dharma sharing guidelines before the reading. Some facilitators prefer “popcorn” style or to go around the circle, each friend mentioning one.. Reassure us that it’s ok to practice Deep Listening if that feels comfortable. Also, you might mention that if we feel nervous and maybe our heart is beating strongly, that could be our body inviting us to open our heart and share.

Shared Reading. It’s wise to invest time practice with the text you select, aware of how its content lands in you. Readings provide an opportunity for us to deepen our practice so the same may happen in for others. Select a text of a moderate length (1,000 words too long. Depending upon the content, 374 words may be just right.) Practice with the text over several days. Recently, a facilitator offered the prompt “What do you want to let go of?” that grew out of their reading of the text and Buddha’s teaching about releasing cows. Then, when we read the text together, this teaching had a deeper meaning because we’d had a moment to reflect beforehand and to learn from each other.

~55 minutes after the meeting starts

Dharma Sharing

As facilitator, we “hold the space” and we see to it that everyone feels safe and included. We are also timekeepers.

Sometimes there may be a lot of emotion and perhaps tears during a sharing. If the feelings are deep and you can feel the shift in yourself and in sangha, you may invite the bell once, with the guidance “let’s come back to our breathing...” to provide space for everyone to settle.

Sometimes we may get lost in our sharing and ramble, touching many different topics. We may not be aware of how many friends still haven’t shared. As facilitator, you may bow in, or tap the bell gently, and ask the person conclude their sharing. Mention that we want everyone to have a chance to share.

~75 minutes after the meeting starts

Closing Dharma Sharing. About 5 minutes before the close, please announce. “We have a few minutes, if you’d like to share this would be a good time.” “If there’s anyone who hasn’t had a chance to share....”

~80 minutes after the meeting starts

Metta Circle

Open the Metta Circle by sharing “dear friends, our Metta Circle is now open. You may wish to place someone in it so the sangha can hold and support them by mentioning their name and, if you wish, what they are struggling with.”

Close the Metta Circle with 1 bell

~85 minutes after the meeting starts

Announcements

~89 minutes after the meeting starts

Closing Wake up the bell and invite two bells close together. Then remind us you’ll invite 3 bells—the first to stand, the second to bow to the sangha, remembering to have the circle include our zoom friends and the 3rd to Share the Merit.

“May the fruits of our practice benefit all beings and the earth”

The meeting ends (~90 minutes after the start)

Invite friends to stay and to visit!

This is an example for a 90-minute Sangha meeting. Adjust the intervals to the length and needs of your gathering. See also Facilitating a Sangha Meeting.

Adapted from Flow of the Meeting & Bell Cadences by Janey Gilman. Source revision: 2.21 rev 2.23, 5.24. Clock times have been converted to an example schedule relative to the meeting start.
