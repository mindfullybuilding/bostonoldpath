Sangha Facilitation Overview

Sangha facilitation is an expanding opportunity to deepen our own practice; to practice inclusiveness; and to accept people who are difficult for us, with no expectation that they’ll ever change. It asks us to be patient with our self. When we practice inclusiveness, we can observe our capacity to love expand. While there’s no one way to facilitate, there are definitely things we have learned from our experience and which Thầy and the Plum Village monastics share with us. As facilitators, we try to create a container in which people feel safe to open their heart and to deepen their practice. “If you want to deliver honey to someone,” Thầy has said, “you need a container.” Our form, tradition and rituals are the container.

Sangha offers an opportunity to connect authentically with others and to feel safe enough to look at our own suffering in order to change it. We’ve learned “what we don’t transform, we transmit.” We each take in the Dharma through our own filters and, happily, it manifests in each of us differently. Thầy writes we are all different flowers. When we come together and cross pollinate, we each bloom and create a beautiful garden. As we deepen our practice, we develop our skillfulness and support our friends to share what’s true for them.

The most important thing for us when we facilitate is to model mindfulness for the sangha. This means being present body and mind together, being calm and relaxed. On the day we facilitate we try to set aside at least a few minutes before sangha opens in order to create ease and joy in our self. Then we are ready to invite the first bell.

Who Can Facilitate?

Almost everyone can facilitate, of course, with training and practice. But generally, facilitators are those who seek to deepen their practice by regular attendance and who participate in other practice opportunities. Many of us have also taken the Five Mindfulness Trainings, and everyone is encouraged to do so. We seek to embody and model the Plum Village Tradition. A facilitator is someone who--

~ Is an experienced practitioner, familiar with our tradition

~ Practices regularly with us and is familiar with Peaceful Garden Sangha format and culture

~ Takes time to be familiar with the material they will be sharing

~ When sharing a personal experience, focuses on how the practice relates to their personal experience and shares the way the practice helped to transform their suffering.

Introduction to The Evening.

It’s a good practice to notice when there’s a newcomer. We might begin by asking if this is the first time for anyone. If so, it will feel welcoming to offer a brief introduction to meditation and our tradition so they may feel comfortable with us.

You might mention that Thich Nhat Hanh has no interest in people labeling themselves “Buddhist” and he advises that if you have a strong connection to a root tradition, not to cut yourself off at the roots from it. Everyone can use the practices we offer to strengthen our connection with our root tradition because teachings in this tradition are complementary to other traditions.

About the Dharma Sharing Guidelines

Thầy’s teaching on how to speak mindfully and how to listen to others are among his great gifts to the Sangha. And beyond. They lie at the heart of our community, creating a calm, receptive environment where everyone can feel safe. Because the guidelines promote safety and trust we often share things in sangha that we don’t share with family or close friends. We learn to be respectful of each other and steer away from our reactivity about what we hear.

Dharma Sharing promotes an exploration into the teachings (the Dharma) and the practice. It’s a time to share what we notice in our self with respect to the practice. We may speak about what has moved us or how we apply the teachings in daily life.
