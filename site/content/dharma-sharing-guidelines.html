Dharma Sharing Guidelines

Guidance for caring for our practice together.

Dharma Sharing Guidelines (see the Order of Interbeing website) We base our sharing on our own experience of the practice rather than about abstract ideas and theoretical topics. Overtime, we realize that many of us share similar difficulties and aspirations. Sitting, listening and sharing together, we recognize our connection to one another.

Please remember that whatever is shared during the Dharma discussion time is confidential. If a friend shares about a difficulty, we respect that they may or may not wish to talk about this individually outside of the Dharma discussion time. Sometimes, just being able to share relieves a lot of suffering. Please ask if it’s ok to mention what that person shared before initiating a conversation with them.

You may wish to say that no one needs to share and that their presence and deep listening already benefit the sangha. You might remind us that Dharma sharing is a time to practice the Fourth Mindfulness Training.

1. We slow down. We remember to take a breath before speaking and signal we’d like to speak. We follow our breathing when we speak and take our time.

2. We bow in and bow out and say our name, before we speak. This practice fosters a sense of inclusion for newcomers as well as supporting “regulars” who might forget a name. No one will interrupt us and we will not interrupt anyone.

3. We share with the whole sangha and don’t engage in crosstalk (respond to) with anyone nor exclude anyone. We speak to the entire circle. We can mention practices that have been helpful to us, but we don’t refer to anyone by name or to what they shared.

4. We share from our hearts and personal experience We reflect on how we apply the teachings in daily life. We can speak about what has moved us in the reading or anything that arises. Since we are sharing from the heart, we don’t rehearse so we don’t always know what we’ll say.

5. We speak about our own mindfulness practice. We share our personal joys, concerns, challenges and insights of our own practice.

6. We practice deep listening and skillful speech. Dharma Sharing is equally about skillful speech and deep listening so we are fully present for the person who is sharing. We focus on understanding the person speaking and listen so deeply that we hear what is being said as well as what is not being said. We listen more than we speak.

7. We observe our reactivity when it arises and don’t respond to what someone else says. If a response or some reactivity arises, we wait for a while to let it sink in and respond only later-- if at all. If a strong emotion arises during sharing, the facilitator might say “let’s come back to our breath” and invite the bell. We can enjoy three breaths to allow calm and mindfulness to reestablish.

8. We aim to be of “lean of expression” and mindful of the length of our sharing in relation to the group size. Everyone should have the chance to speak so we speak not too much and not too little. Near the end the facilitator may offer an opportunity for those who have not spoken to do so if they wish and may address any unanswered questions.

9. We avoid speaking a second time until everyone who wants to share has spoken. 10. We avoid giving advice even if it’s asked for.

11. All that arises is confidential--what is said here stays here.

12. We maintain a “double” confidentiality about what is said in the group. While we speak freely to the group we may not want to be reminded of what we said or want to discuss it afterwards. We don’t approach anyone about what they shared without first asking permission. Confidentiality secures the safety of the group and helps avoid gossiping. After sangha, if we want to talk to with someone about what they said in the group, we first ask their permission. Sometimes people share their suffering simply to have it be held by the Sangha and they really don’t want to talk about it. We show our respect when we ask permission.

13. It’s always OK not to share. If you notice that it’s become a habit not to share during sangha, you may want to be curious about why and explore the reasons with yourself or with a friend.

Transcribed from Dharma Sharing Guidelines by Janey Gilman. Source revision: 2.19 rev 2.21, 2.25, 4.26.
