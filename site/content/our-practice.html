Mindfulness woven into ordinary life.

We practice returning to the present moment with awareness and kindness — in meditation, in community, and in the everyday acts of living.

Everyday practice.

Mindfulness means being aware of what is happening in the present moment. We can pause, notice the breath, and bring attention to the body and the world around us.

In the Plum Village tradition, mindfulness is part of daily life. Washing dishes, making tea, walking to work, and listening to another person can all become opportunities to practice.

Sitting and breathing

We take time to settle and attend to the breath. Guided and silent meditation are ways to become familiar with our experience.

Walking and arriving

We bring attention to each step and our contact with the earth. Walking can be a practice of returning to this moment.

Listening and sharing

We practice listening with care and speaking from our own experience. Dharma sharing helps us explore the teachings in everyday life.

Practice with a sangha.

A community of practice can nourish our capacity to be present. Groups may include meditation, readings, songs, and sharing. Each group has its own format and arrangements.
